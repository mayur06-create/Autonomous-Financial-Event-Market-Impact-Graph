India CPI Inflation Rises to 6.8% in August, Breaching RBI Upper Tolerance Band
Business Standard, September 12, 2023

NEW DELHI — India's retail inflation, measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI),
surged to 6.83% year-on-year in August 2023, breaching the Reserve Bank of India's
upper tolerance band of 6% for the first time in five months, driven primarily by a
spike in vegetable prices (tomato +300% YoY) and elevated cereals inflation.

Core CPI (excluding food and fuel) remained relatively contained at 4.9%, providing
some comfort to policymakers. However, the headline print significantly exceeded the
consensus estimate of 6.2% and the RBI's own projection of 5.4% for Q2 FY24.

Implications:
- RBI policy: The print makes a September rate cut virtually impossible. Market
  participants shifted expectations of the first cut to Q1 FY25. Overnight index
  swap (OIS) rates moved 8–10 bps higher.
- Bond market: The 10-year government securities yield rose 6 bps to 7.19% on
  inflation fears. A higher-for-longer stance implies sustained bond supply.
- Banking sector: Deposit rate competition is likely to remain high; margins under
  pressure for liability-heavy banks.
- FMCG companies (HUL, Nestle, Dabur): Input cost pressures from food inflation;
  rural demand likely to be hurt by food price inflation eating into disposable income.
- Agriculture-linked stocks: Agro-chemicals, fertilizers, and tractor companies
  (M&M) may see mixed impact — demand for agri-inputs rises but farmer income is
  volatile.
- Fixed income: Short-duration funds preferred; long-duration funds face mark-to-
  market losses as yields rise.
