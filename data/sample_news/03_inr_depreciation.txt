Rupee Hits Record Low of 84.50 Against Dollar Amid Global Risk-Off
Mint, October 19, 2024

MUMBAI — The Indian rupee touched an all-time intraday low of 84.50 against the US
dollar on Friday, driven by broad dollar strength after stronger-than-expected US
jobs data raised fears that the Federal Reserve would keep rates higher for longer.

The USD Index (DXY) climbed to 103.8, its highest since August. Foreign portfolio
investors (FPIs) pulled out a net Rs 4,300 crore from domestic equities in the week,
adding to the selling pressure on the rupee.

Key effects:
- Importers (crude oil, edible oil, electronics): Rising input costs in rupee terms.
  Every 1% INR depreciation raises the crude import bill by approximately Rs 12,000
  crore annually (RBI working paper, 2022).
- IT exporters (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL Tech): Positive revenue tailwind; a weaker
  rupee boosts realization from USD-denominated contracts. Industry body NASSCOM
  estimates ~40–50 bps improvement in EBIT margin per 1% INR depreciation.
- Pharma exporters: Similar tailwind to IT; US-generics business benefits.
- Metals (Hindalco, Tata Steel): Mixed. Import cost of coking coal rises; but export
  realizations also improve.
- Gold: Domestic prices in INR terms rise with rupee weakness, dampening demand but
  boosting returns for holders.

The RBI intervened in the forward market to prevent disorderly depreciation. Foreign
exchange reserves stood at $688 billion, providing a comfortable buffer. Analysts
expect the central bank to tolerate modest depreciation but resist a sharp move.
